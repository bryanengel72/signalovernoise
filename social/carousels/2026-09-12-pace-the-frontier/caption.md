## Caption

Anthropic's CEO just asked the whole industry to slow down. The slowdown is not the interesting part.

Dario Amodei's post lays out three ways to "pace the frontier." Two of them need governments and competitors to cooperate. One of them Anthropic is doing alone, starting now: third-party evaluators from groups like METR get a badge, a desk, a laptop, and access "mostly comparable" to the company's own risk teams.

That is the bank examiner model. The regulator sits with the staff instead of reading the quarterly report.

Twenty years in the Air Force taught me the difference. A report tells you what the organization decided to write down. A resident inspector sees what happens on a Tuesday afternoon when nobody is preparing for a visit.

Critics are calling it regulatory capture, since a plan like this mostly benefits the labs already out front. That can be true at the same time. The test is not the announcement. It is what "mostly comparable" turns out to mean once the evaluators are inside.

Sam Altman says OpenAI will do the same.

Which would you trust more, the report or the person inside?
